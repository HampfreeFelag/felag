## Introduction

On November 25, 2026, Royal Decree-Law 44/2026 was published in Spain's Official State Gazette (BOE). The decree grants Spain's financial intelligence unit, Sepblac, the authority to freeze cryptocurrency, bank, and payment accounts without a prior court order. The measure takes effect on January 25, 2027. Here is a breakdown of the legal mechanism and its implications.

## What the decree says

Royal Decree-Law 44/2026 provides Sepblac — Spain's financial intelligence unit for anti-money laundering — with the following powers:

1. **Account freeze up to 5 business days.** Sepblac can block crypto, bank, and payment accounts upon suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing without a judicial warrant.
2. **Transaction halt up to 10 business days.** Individual pending transfers may be suspended for a longer period.
3. **No prior court approval required.** The decision is administrative. After the set period, judicial confirmation or extension is required.
4. **Applies to all providers.** Not only Spanish platforms but also foreign ones providing services to Spanish residents (under AML legislation interpretation).

After 5 days of freeze, a court order is required for extension. The decree also requires convalidation (parliamentary validation) within the statutory timeframe.

## Context: AMLD6 and EU implementation

The decree is part of the implementation of the sixth anti-money laundering directive (AMLD6, Directive 2024/1640). MiCA regulates stablecoin issuance and platform licensing, but financial crime investigation remains the responsibility of national financial intelligence units.

Sepblac — Spain's equivalent of FinCEN (US) or national FIUs (other EU countries) — coordinates anti-money laundering efforts and liaises with international counterparts. Prior to 44/2026, the unit lacked a direct instrument for forced crypto-asset freezing.

## What this means for traders

1. **User risks.** Accounts may be frozen by an administrative decision of Sepblac — without the ability to contest promptly for up to 5 days.
2. **Compliance pressure.** Platforms operating in Spain may restrict access for residents to reduce regulatory exposure.
3. **EU precedent.** The Spanish approach may become a model for other countries implementing AMLD6.
4. **DEX unaffected.** Decentralized platforms have no jurisdiction and no centralized accounts to freeze. The measure targets licensed providers.

## Summary

Royal Decree-Law 44/2026 is one of the harshest national crypto-control instruments in the EU. Sepblac has gained the power to freeze accounts without a court order, but for a limited period with mandatory judicial confirmation. The measure takes effect on January 25, 2027, and implements AMLD6 rather than constituting an independent regulatory step.

## FAQ

**Is account freeze the same as confiscation?**
No. Freeze is a temporary measure lasting up to 5 business days. Confiscation requires a court order and is a separate procedure.

**How to appeal a freeze?**
The decree allows for administrative challenge. After 5 days, judicial confirmation is required to maintain the freeze.

**Will this affect decentralized platforms?**
Likely not. DEXs have no jurisdiction and no centralized accounts to freeze. The measure targets licensed providers.

**What is convalidation?**
It is the parliamentary validation procedure for royal decree-laws. In Spain, decree-laws take immediate effect but must be approved by parliament within a statutory timeframe.
